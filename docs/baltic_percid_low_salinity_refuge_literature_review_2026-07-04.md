# Low-salinity refugia and marine predation pressure on Baltic percids — literature review

- **Date:** 2026-07-04. **Method:** multi-agent deep-research workflow — 5 parallel angle-searches (scite + web + ICES/HELCOM) → adversarial refute-pass on the load-bearing claims → opus synthesis (which invoked the scientific-validation skill). Contested numbers are flagged inline rather than dropped. One verify-agent errored and one returned a degenerate placeholder; the affected claims (cod-reproduction salinity floor; restoration efficacy) were independently covered by other agents and by the peer-reviewed sources cited in §2/§5, so coverage is intact.

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **The core pattern is real and well documented.** Along the ~1300 km, ~12-fold Baltic salinity gradient there is a genuine, quantified marine→freshwater community turnover: marine fish species richness falls ~8–11× from Skagerrak/Kattegat (29–32 psu) to Bothnian Bay (~2.7 psu), while freshwater-origin richness (which includes percids) rises 2–4× (Koehler et al., 2022; **verdict: supported**).
- **But the mechanism is reproductive, not somatic.** Baltic cod are salinity-gated at *spawning*: spermatozoa activate only ≥11–12 psu (Eastern Baltic) / ≥15–16 psu (Belt Sea), and eggs reach neutral buoyancy at ~14.5 psu / ~20–22 psu respectively (Nissling & Westin, 1997). Percid early life stages mirror this at the opposite end — perch larvae fail above ~9.6 ppt; pikeperch eggs/larvae cope only below ~5 psu. So the two taxa's *recruitment* envelopes barely overlap.
- **Adult cod are NOT strictly excluded from low salinity.** Cod persist and feed in the Åland Sea at 7–7.5 psu bottom salinity (sustained by larval drift/immigration, not local recruitment), and that paper explicitly reports "no evidence that extremely low salinity provides predator refuge" (Bergström et al., 2025). This directly weakens a blanket "cod cannot enter the percid zone to feed" framing.
- **The dominant fish-on-percid predation is euryhaline-on-euryhaline, not marine.** Perch (brackish stock tolerant to ~17.5 psu) and pikeperch (euryhaline; adults tolerate well beyond ambient coastal salinity) drive intraguild predation on percids; no genuinely marine fish predator surfaced as a documented, quantified predator of coastal perch/pikeperch. The heaviest quantified top-down pressure comes from **cormorants and seals — birds/mammals, not salinity-limited fish**.
- **Net implication for OSMOSE:** salinity refugia are a mechanism to get the *spatial* distribution of predation right, not a guaranteed cure for percid overshoot (see §8).

## 2. Physiological/ecological basis

**Cod reproductive salinity floors (high confidence).** Nissling & Westin (1997, MEPS 152:261) establish population-specific thresholds: spermatozoa activation ≥11–12 psu (Baltic/Bornholm stock) vs ≥15–16 psu (Belt Sea stock); neutral egg buoyancy at 14.5 ± 1.2 psu (Baltic) vs 20–22 psu (Belt Sea). Because eggs must stay suspended above the anoxic/benthic layer, this restricts *successful spawning* to deep, saline sub-halocline basins — presently chiefly the Bornholm Basin for Eastern Baltic cod (Frontiers review, 2023).

> **Citation-accuracy flag (from verification):** some source notes attribute these thresholds to "Nissling & Vallin 1996, MEPS 152:261." The adversarial check found this is a **conflation of two real papers**. The numbers belong to **Nissling & Westin (1997)**, *MEPS* 152:261–271 (doi:10.3354/meps152261). **Nissling & Vallin (1996)**, *J. Fish Biol.* 48:217–227 (doi:10.1111/j.1095-8649.1996.tb01114.x) is a separate, real paper on egg buoyancy maintenance. Cite the 1997 MEPS paper for the thresholds.

**This is a reproductive constraint, not proof of adult foraging avoidance (high confidence).** The strongest single piece of evidence is Bergström et al. (2025, *ICES J. Mar. Sci.*, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsaf197): cod occur in spawning condition in the Åland Sea at 7–7.5 psu, but lab fertilization collapses from 86% (15 psu) → 3% (10 psu) → 0.4% (7.5 psu). The population is upheld by northward larval transport / adult migration to higher-salinity spawning grounds, **not** local reproduction — and the authors explicitly state the low-salinity zone is a well-oxygenated, food-rich feeding habitat, *not* a predator refuge. **This is the key caveat against an over-strong "salinity excludes cod" model assumption.**

**Percids are the mirror image — but only in early life (high/medium confidence).**
- **Perch:** brackish-adapted *Perca fluviatilis* tolerate up to ~17.5 psu as juveniles/adults (via combined hyper-/hypo-osmoregulation) vs only ~10 psu for freshwater-origin perch (Christensen et al., 2019, doi:10.1093/conphys/coz004). Note the verification caveat: at 17.5 psu half of individuals reached loss of equilibrium within ~3 weeks, so this is a *stressed survivable ceiling*, not a comfortable range. Perch larvae fail above ~9.6 ppt (verified via doi:10.1007/BF00877201: only 2/344 larvae survived at 9.6 ppt).
- **Pikeperch:** adults tolerate acute transfer to at least ~16 psu with stable hypo-osmoregulation (Brown, 2001, doi:10.1006/jfbi.2001.1800), and are widely described as euryhaline. Their *eggs/larvae* cope only below ~5 psu.

> **⚠️ Contested numbers (down-ranked):** The frequently-quoted pikeperch figures "egg survival highest at 0.7 psu, total mortality by 6.7 psu" and "adult pikeperch tolerant to ~30 psu" could **not be traced to a verifiable primary source** in verification. The 0.7–6.7 psu range recurs in secondary summaries (attributed to Klinkhardt & Winkler 1989 via Lehtonen's review) but no resolvable DOI was confirmed; the "~30 psu" ceiling traces mainly to grey/aquaculture sources. **Treat the direction (pikeperch eggs are oligohaline-restricted; adults are broadly euryhaline) as solid, but treat the exact psu figures as indicative, not established.**

**Bottom line:** the actual asymmetry is **reproductive/recruitment-stage**, not adult-somatic. A defensible refuge argument centres on (a) recruitment-stage salinity partitioning and (b) *lower adult cod densities* in truly oligohaline nearshore zones (<~5 psu: Curonian Lagoon mouth, inner Gulf of Riga, Bothnian Bay) — **not** a blanket adult-cod-avoidance rule across the whole 7–11 psu band.

## 3. Empirical spatial segregation in the Baltic

- **Community turnover (supported).** Koehler et al. (2022, *Biogeosciences*, doi:10.5194/bg-19-2295-2022): over a ~1300 km / 12-fold gradient (2.68–32.40 psu, Table 1), marine SR_std falls 8–11× (39–57 spp. in Skagerrak/Kattegat → 5 in Bothnian Bay); freshwater SR_std rises 2–4×; salinity is framed as an "ecological barrier." Fully verified against the published PDF.
  > **Corrections from verification:** (1) the 2–4× rise is for *all* freshwater-origin fish, **not** a percid-specific metric — perch/pikeperch are named only illustratively. (2) The claim that "marine migratory species are restricted above ~9 psu" is a **misattribution**: the "~9" figure in Koehler et al. actually describes a *freshwater*-fish tolerance limit (sourced to a non-Baltic US study), not a marine-restriction threshold. Do not use "~9 psu" as a marine-predator cutoff. (3) "Bothnian Bay/Sea <3 psu" is imprecise — only Bothnian Bay (2.68) is <3; Bothnian Sea is ~5.1 psu.
- **Eastern vs Western cod stock zoning (high confidence).** Stock interaction is possible only in western spawning areas where salinity satisfies both stocks; low salinity in eastern areas prevents Belt Sea cod spawning (Nissling & Westin, 1997) — reinforcing that cod reproduction occupies a specific higher-salinity envelope.
- **Pikeperch spawning confinement (supported).** Pikeperch are essentially restricted to shallow, sheltered, low-salinity (<~5 psu) coastal bays, estuaries and archipelago waters structurally unsuitable for cod reproduction (Ložys/Olsson, 2019, *Fishes*, doi:10.3390/fishes4010007). The "structural segregation between the two groups" is a sound synthesis of two well-established literatures rather than a single directly-stated joint finding.
- **Predator/prey coastal gradient (medium confidence).** Along ~1200 km of Swedish coast, the boundary between predator-dominated (perch/pike) and stickleback-dominated bays moved from ~8 km (1996) to ~21 km (2014) offshore (doi:10.1038/s42003-020-01180-0) — but wave exposure and latitude, not salinity per se, were the stronger statistical drivers here.
- **Climate framing (medium confidence).** A Western Baltic vulnerability assessment rates cod as higher-risk and percids (perch/pike/pikeperch) as comparative "winners" under freshening (doi:10.1038/s41598-024-67029-2). Note: this paper received a **published correction** (not a retraction; doi:10.1038/s41598-025-04750-6); the correction's specific content was not directly read, so treat as medium confidence.
- **HELCOM HOLAS 3 (low confidence, indirect).** HOLAS 3 institutionally separates "coastal fish" assessment units from "open sea" commercial-stock units, consistent with structurally distinct nearshore/low-salinity vs open-sea communities — but the queried indicator layer was *cyprinids*, not Percidae, so this is only indirect support.

## 4. Which marine predators are salinity-excluded

**Marine fish (salinity-limited, ~>11 psu):**
- **Atlantic cod** — the only genuinely marine piscivore of note. Its *reproduction* is salinity/oxygen-gated to deep basins (§2). Its documented Baltic diet is dominated by sprat, herring and round goby (round goby up to 100% occurrence where abundant), **not coastal percids**.
  > **Verification caveat:** the "cod is *salinity-excluded as a coastal percid predator*" framing is **only partially supported**. The abiotic thresholds and basin names are correct, but no retrieved source tests or states a cod→percid predator-exclusion mechanism; adult/juvenile cod have far broader salinity tolerance than eggs, and some sources list cod among "typical coastal predatory fish." Treat cod's absence from the coastal percid-predator role as an inference consistent with habitat partitioning, not a demonstrated exclusion mechanism.
- **Turbot, garfish** — present as coastal marine/euryhaline piscivores, but no diet study links either to perch/pikeperch predation (diets center on gobies, sandeel, sprat). Low confidence, negative result.

**Euryhaline predators — NOT excluded (and these dominate the fish-on-percid pathway):**
- **Pikeperch** — euryhaline; the dominant documented fish predator implicated in structuring perch populations (doi:10.1007/s10750-019-04008-z). This is an intraguild/euryhaline interaction, **not** a marine-predator effect (verdict: partially-supported — the ecological logic is well supported; the specific "~30 psu" tolerance number is not primary-verified).
- **Perch** itself — brackish stock tolerant to ~17.5 psu, so low coastal salinity does not exclude it.

**Non-fish top predators (record separately — this framing is *supported*):**
- **Great cormorant** — the single best-quantified predator: perch median 4–10% (up to 21%) annual predation mortality (Heikinheimo et al., 2022, doi:10.1093/icesjms/fsab258); pikeperch instantaneous mortality 0.04–0.13/yr, ages 2–4 most vulnerable (Salmi et al., 2015, doi:10.1139/cjfas-2015-0033).
- **Grey seal** — documented dietary predator of perch/pikeperch (perch in ~78%, pikeperch ~67% of some samples), but evidence is diet-proportion, not population-mortality rate.

**Key point:** cormorants and seals operate *inside* the low-salinity coastal zone. A salinity refuge shelters percids from *marine fish* predators (essentially only cod, and only weakly), **not** from the avian/mammalian predators that actually dominate quantified percid mortality.

## 5. Management/restoration angle

Coastal low-salinity refugia are an **evidence-backed** management lever, but a multi-stressor one:

- **Restoration works, measurably (high confidence).** Björnöfjärden P-binding restoration produced a monotonic perch increase 2011–2020 vs reference (doi:10.1007/s13280-023-01907-3); wetland-adjacent bays hold ~90% higher pike abundance and >10× higher larval emigration (doi:10.1016/j.ecss.2018.02.014; doi:10.1007/s10750-023-05216-4).
- **Fishing-closure (no-take) areas (high confidence).** 2–3× higher piscivore and 2–7× higher perch biomass than fished areas (doi:10.1007/s13280-018-1133-9) — fishing removal is an independent lever, not just water clarity.
- **Early-life salinity sensitivity is the mechanistic basis for nursery refugia (high confidence).** Perch/pike hatching is similar in freshwater and ~7 ppt, but fry survival/growth drop sharply above ~6–8 psu; juvenile body weight is reduced ~50% at 10 psu vs freshwater after 130 days (doi:10.1111/j.1600-0633.2011.00537.x).
- **Bay enclosure limits stickleback access (high confidence).** More enclosed "flads" better shelter percid recruitment because stickleback (a major egg/larval predator) access is limited by openings to the sea (doi:10.1016/j.ecss.2015.06.017; doi:10.1007/s13280-015-0665-5).
- **Refuge protection does not remove predation (medium confidence).** Cormorants/seals operate inside refugia; the cormorant-mortality figures above are corroborated by the peer-reviewed cormorant papers in §4.
- **Opposite signs for the two percids (medium confidence).** Restoring water clarity to BSAP reference is modelled to raise perch ~37% but cut pikeperch ~59% (doi:10.1007/s13280-019-01263-1) — "protecting the refuge" is not uniformly good for the whole percid guild; pikeperch needs turbidity.

## 6. Modeling angle — how to represent it

**Precedent in other spatially-explicit ecosystem models:**
- **Baltic Atlantis (Bossier et al., 2018, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0199168)** tracks salinity per box/depth-layer but uses it **only** as a scalar on the Beverton–Holt recruitment relationship — **not** as a predation-accessibility driver (and it disables the effect entirely for seals/porpoises). So the mechanism is *unused* in the current Baltic build.
- **Generic Atlantis (Audzijonyte et al., 2019, doi:10.1111/2041-210x.13272)** *does* architecturally support it: predation runs through a stage-structured availability matrix in a modified Holling type-II function, further limited by time-varying spatial overlap, predator condition, **"available habitat for refuge,"** and gape — any of which could be conditioned on a salinity-derived term.
- **Ecopath-with-Ecospace (Christensen et al., 2014, doi:10.1007/s10021-014-9803-3)** is the most directly transferable: its habitat-capacity model multiplies per-cell environmental suitability functions (depth, bottom type, temperature, **salinity**, oxygen) per life stage, scaling foraging capacity and interacting with foraging-arena vulnerability. **Salinity response curves per life stage are already a first-class named input.**
- **Empirical Baltic precedent (Neuenfeldt & Beyer, 2003, doi:10.1046/j.1095-8649.2003.00013.x):** cod–clupeid overlap volume in the Bornholm Basin is set by salinity stratification and oxygenation — i.e., realized predation accessibility is *already* salinity/oxygen-gated in the real system.

**Concrete OSMOSE mechanism.** OSMOSE has the exact insertion points:
1. The **accessibility/catchability CSV matrix** (predator columns × prey rows, size-class-splittable via the `<` upper-bound syntax, e.g. `cod<0.25`,`cod<2`,`cod`) caps the fraction of each prey available to each predator.
2. **Per-species-per-stage seasonal distribution/movement maps** drive predator–prey spatial overlap (commonly built externally via GAMs on temperature, **salinity**, chl-a, oxygen, bathymetry — Grüss et al., 2015, doi:10.1016/j.jmarsys.2014.11.004).

The proposed mechanism: make the cod→percid accessibility value (or the cod distribution-map weighting) a **per-cell function of the forced salinity field** — precisely the salinity/RV forcing already present in the OSMOSE-Baltic repo. Low-salinity cells drop cod's access to percid prey toward zero; higher-salinity cells restore it. This encodes the *spatial* predation structure that Neuenfeldt & Beyer show is real, using architecture OSMOSE already has.

## 7. What could be done — ranked options

### (a) Real-world / management

1. **Protect and restore enclosed, vegetated, low-salinity coastal nursery bays** (reed/wetland re-flooding, enclosure of flads). *Evidence: strong* (multiple peer-reviewed restoration + recruitment studies).
2. **Maintain/expand no-take or gear-restricted areas in nursery habitat.** *Evidence: strong* (2–7× perch biomass effect).
3. **Manage stickleback-driven top-down pressure** (protect enclosed bays that limit stickleback access; consider targeted stickleback exploitation). *Evidence: strong for the mechanism.*
4. **Manage cormorant/seal predation within refugia** where local percid recovery is a goal. *Evidence: medium* (predation quantified; management efficacy less so).
5. **Species-specific targeting:** clarity restoration favors perch but harms pikeperch — set the water-clarity target to the prioritized species. *Evidence: medium* (single modelling study).

### (b) MODEL representation (OSMOSE)

1. **Salinity-dependent predation *accessibility* gating the predation kernel per cell** — the cod→percid accessibility multiplier as a function of the in-repo salinity/RV field. Cleanest, most targeted; matches EwE habitat-capacity precedent and Neuenfeldt & Beyer empirics. *Evidence: strong architectural precedent; medium ecological (cod-adult exclusion is soft — see §8).*
2. **Salinity-conditioned cod distribution/movement maps** (GAM-style occupancy vs salinity) so cod schools simply don't occupy oligohaline nearshore cells. Complementary to option 1; matches standard OSMOSE map-forcing. *Evidence: strong precedent.*
3. **Salinity-gated percid *recruitment/early-life* survival** (the better-supported physiological axis) rather than adult-predation gating alone — encode perch-larvae failure >~9.6 ppt and pikeperch egg confinement <~5 psu. *Evidence: strong physiological basis; note pikeperch exact psu numbers are indicative.*
4. **Add the non-fish predators (cormorant/seal) as explicit coastal mortality terms** so the refuge does not spuriously zero out percid mortality. *Evidence: strong — these dominate quantified percid mortality.*

## 8. Caveats & confidence

- **Direction-of-effect honesty (the central tension).** If OSMOSE currently lets marine predators (cod) *over-access* percids in low-salinity cells, adding a salinity refuge will **raise** percid biomass. That makes this a mechanism to get the **spatial predation pattern right**, and — if percids are currently *under*-predicted — plausibly helpful. But the project's standing problem is percid **overshoot** (×38–96 in Baltic runs). A refuge that reduces cod predation on percids would, if anything, **worsen** overshoot, not cure it. **This mechanism should be framed as spatial-realism correction, not an overshoot fix.** (Prior project diagnostics attribute overshoot to structural/grid and missing self-limiters — thermal recruitment gating, stickleback/cormorant predation, cannibalism — not to excess cod predation.)
- **The "cod excluded from low salinity" premise is soft (contested).** Bergström et al. (2025) directly state there is *no evidence* that extremely low salinity provides a predator refuge, and that adult cod feed well at 7–7.5 psu. The exclusion is **reproductive**, not foraging. Any accessibility gate should therefore act on *very low* salinity (<~5 psu) nearshore zones and/or be modest across the 7–11 psu band, not a hard cutoff.
- **Misattributed/inverted numbers to avoid:** (1) the "~9 psu marine-migratory restriction" figure is a misattributed freshwater-tolerance number from a non-Baltic study — do not use it as a cod cutoff. (2) Bothnian Sea is ~5 psu, not <3. (3) Cite Nissling & **Westin** (1997) for the cod thresholds, not "Nissling & Vallin 1996."
- **Unverified primary sources (down-ranked):** pikeperch egg-survival 0.7–6.7 psu and pikeperch adult ~30 psu tolerance are **not** confirmed against retrievable primary literature — use as indicative only.
- **Correction (not retraction) noted:** the 2024 Western Baltic vulnerability assessment (doi:10.1038/s41598-024-67029-2) has a published correction whose content was not directly read.
- **HELCOM/HOLAS 3 support is indirect** (cyprinid indicator, not Percidae).
- **No retractions** were found among the verified peer-reviewed sources.
